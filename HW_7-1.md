## What does Jev's claim of no hallucinations really mean?

Jev's claim refers to its constrained output rather than a guarantee of factual correctness. Unlike traditional LLMs that generate free-form text, Jev returns structured decisions such as Boolean probabilities, categorical choices, or scores. Because its outputs are restricted to predefined types and options, it cannot fabricate arbitrary explanations, diagnoses, or information outside those outputs. This reminds me of when we experimented with the Llama LLM in class, where it sometimes made up answers that were not necessarily supported by the information provided.

However, the absence of hallucinations does not imply the absence of errors. Jev can still misinterpret input, select the wrong category, or assign misleading probabilities. Its reliability therefore depends on the quality of the input, the questions being asked, and the validation of its decisions.

## How would I apply Jev to my use case?

In my TriageFlow application, I use Jev to interpret a fictional patient's natural-language responses and convert them into structured findings.

For example, when a patient reports chest tightness, Jev estimates the probability that the patient is experiencing chest pain. My application then uses predefined thresholds to classify the finding as present, absent, or unknown.

These structured findings are passed to a deterministic Python rule engine that determines whether the fictional patient should be directed toward emergency care, urgent care, or primary care.

This architecture takes advantage of Jev's constrained outputs while maintaining explicit, reviewable rules for triage. Importantly, Jev does not independently diagnose patients or determine their final care setting.

Because incorrect classification remains possible, I would test Jev against labeled patient scenarios, evaluate its probability thresholds, and preserve uncertain responses rather than assuming they are negative findings.

Beyond my TriageFlow application, I could see Jev being useful in customer support systems. For example, when a customer describes a problem, Jev could classify it into predefined categories such as billing, technical support, or account management. The application could then route the request to the appropriate department.
